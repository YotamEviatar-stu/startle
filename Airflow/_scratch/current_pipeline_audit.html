Your pipeline, stage by stage — and where it leaks

Ten stages as the code actually executes them. Seven are sound. Every number below came from running run_glm_scoring on the existing cache, not from reading the code and guessing.

The finding

Trial loss: 16.44% Evening vs 24.28% Morning  p = 2.5e-04

Morning loses half again as many trials as Evening, and it is not because Morning data is worse. Two of the ten rejection gates count breaths against a fixed number inside a fixed-length window — and breathing rate is itself one of the things that differs between the two conditions (median period 3.320 s Evening vs 3.640 s Morning, p = 2.6e-136). A slower breather puts fewer breaths in the same 23 s window and fails the count. The gate is reading respiration rate and reporting it as data quality.

This matters beyond bookkeeping: the trials that survive in Morning are the faster-breathing ones, so the two conditions are no longer being compared on the same kind of trial.

Where every gate fires

Rejection rate per gate, Evening bar above Morning bar, with the two-proportion p on the right. Only low_information and no_cycles_found are significantly imbalanced — and they are exactly the two that count breaths. noisy_baseline is the second-largest gate overall (6.58% vs 5.73%) but is balanced (p = 0.57).

The remaining gates are nearly inert on this cohort: flat_signal 2/5, atypical_shape 4/0, flat_response 0/3, low_coverage 2/1, rate_artifact 0/1 trials. They carry design risk — two of them condition on the trial’s own response — but they are not what is distorting this dataset.

1
Layer 1 — load and cachemeasured here at 25 Hz

Read the recording once, keep a 25 Hz copy so nothing has to touch the raw file again. Trial times come from the DIN triggers and are stored in seconds, so they survive any later change of sample rate.

airflow_glm.py:33 process_session
butter(2) LP 70 Hz anti-alias, native rate resample -> CACHE_SFREQ = 25 Hz cache: signal_raw, times_raw, trials_meta
This 25 Hz copy is what stage 4 measures RA on. The resample low-passes at 12.5 Hz, so a sharp inspiratory apex is already rounded off before it is ever measured — PsPM Stage 5 measures on the native trace. The fix is in the file but inert: native_envelope() now caches the native running min/max, and it activates on the next FORCE_RELOAD.
AB22 / evening, 27 s of the cached trace. Six breaths.
2
QC excursion bridgesmall in practice

Find brief spikes that are far larger than a breath, and draw a straight line across them. The same spans are later marked missing, so they drop out of the model too.

airflow_qc.py:39 flag_excursions · QC_ENABLED = True
residual vs rolling median > QC_EXCURSION_K (5) x breath_size runs <= QC_MAX_RUN_SEC (1 s) -> replaced by a straight line longer runs left alone; spans also go to missing_spans
The bridged copy is what gets passed on as signal_for_pipeline, so a cycle overlapping a bridge has its RA measured on a fabricated straight line. Cohort-wide this is 130 spans / 22.6 s Evening and 87 / 16.9 s Morning — real, but far too small to move a result. Worth fixing for cleanliness, not urgency.
3
Filter cascade and downsamplematches PsPM

Strip everything that is not breathing — slow baseline wander first, then anything faster than a breath — and drop to 10 Hz. This copy is only used to find when breaths happen.

airflow_glm.py:224 phase1_filter_downsample
mean-centre butter(1) LP 0.6 Hz filtfilt \ two SEPARATE passes butter(1) HP 0.01 Hz filtfilt / resample -> 10 Hz then robust z (median / MAD)
I said earlier that this was a single butter(2, band) and needed changing. That was wrong — the function already cascades two 1st-order filtfilt passes, exactly pspm_resp_pp.m Stage 1. GLM_CYCLE_BANDPASS just holds the two cutoffs. Nothing to do here.
Grey: mean-centred raw, rescaled to overlay. Teal: after both passes and the downsample.
4
Cycle detectionmatches PsPM

Inspiration begins where the cleaned signal falls through zero, so counting crossings counts breaths. Then each breath gets three numbers: duration, depth, and depth over duration.

airflow_glm.py:219 detect_cycles
medfilt 1.0 s falling zero-crossings (bellows rule, not the derivative) refractory 1.0 s, single pass over the original list RP from the 10 Hz clock; RA/RFR from signal_raw
The detection half is faithful, including MATLAB’s single-pass refractory semantics. The measurement half inherits stage 1’s 25 Hz problem — and stage 2’s bridged segments — because it reads whichever copy it was handed.
Grey: detection copy. Teal: after the 1 s median filter. Ticks: one per detected inspiration onset.
5
Deep-breath dropbalanced, but by amplitude

Any breath more than five times deeper than the session’s typical breath is thrown away as an artifact.

airflow_qc.py:81 flag_deep_breaths · QC_MAX_BREATH_RATIO = 5.0
RA > 5.0 x session-median RA -> cycle DROPPED, span marked missing
This rejects on amplitude, and a respiratory startle response is a deep breath — so the gate can in principle delete the thing being measured. In this cohort it fires on 39/9318 Evening (0.42%) and 28/8129 Morning (0.34%), p=0.505: small and balanced. Flagged as a design risk, not a live distortion.
Four minutes of per-breath RA. The line is 5x the session median; dots above it are dropped.
6
Series and session cropmatches PsPM

Turn one number per breath into a continuous line, then tune it to the timescale a response lives on. The filter runs forwards only, so no sample is shaped by the future.

airflow_glm.py:355 build_continuous_series
interpolate knots at assign_time (value pinned to the breath's END) butter(1) [HP per metric, LP 1 Hz], lfilter = causal missing spans NaN'd AFTER filtering, never before
Per-modality high-pass split is correct (RP 0.01 Hz, RA/RFR 0.001 Hz) and the NaN-after-filter ordering is right — blanking first would poison every later sample through the IIR recursion.
The RA series after interpolation and the causal sensitivity filter.
7
Subject-level z-scorematches Bach 2016

Put every subject on a common amplitude scale, because lung size is not reactivity. Pooling across both sessions is what keeps the Evening-versus-Morning difference intact.

airflow_glm.py:403 zscore_subject_series
RA, RFR: pooled over BOTH of a subject's sessions RP: left in seconds, untouched
Per-session z-scoring here would normalise away the contrast the study exists to measure. The code pools correctly.
8
Pass 1 — trial statisticsrelative, no floor

Summarise each trial by how variable it is before and after the stimulus, and by how far its shape sits from the session’s average trial.

airflow_glm.py:948 _run_glm_analysis
baseline_std, response_std per trial shape-centroid MSD across the session's trials cutoff = median(MSD) + 3.0 x robust_sd(MSD)
The shape cutoff is defined relative to the session’s own trials with no absolute floor, so it always removes the most deviant trials whether or not any are bad — and a large startle response is deviant by construction. It fires on 4 Evening / 0 Morning trials here, so the practical damage is small.
9
Pass 2 — the ten rejection gatesthe real problem

Ten independent yes/no tests per trial. Any one of them firing throws the whole trial away.

airflow_glm.py:1082-1160
no_cycles_found n_cycles < 2 OR NK2 finds none low_information n_cycles < QC_MIN_KNOTS (5) noisy_baseline baseline_std z > 3 vs session flat_signal / flat_response std < 0.1 x session median cycle_gap 60/max_gap < 5 bpm rate_artifact > 40 bpm atypical_shape low_coverage amplitude_artifact
Two of the ten count breaths against a fixed number on a fixed-length window — and breathing rate is itself a condition difference. Trial windows are the same length in both conditions (23.10 s Evening vs 23.00 s Morning, p=0.101), but median breath period is 3.320 s Evening vs 3.640 s Morning (p=2.6e-136). So a 23 s window holds 6.9 breaths in Evening and 6.3 in Morning, and a cut at 5 sits inside Morning’s left tail: low_information takes 10.82% of Evening trials and 21.01% of Morning (p=1.5e-07), no_cycles_found another 1.23% vs 4.77% (p=1.4e-04). Overall 16.44% vs 24.28%, p=2.5e-04. The gate is not measuring data quality; it is measuring how fast the subject was breathing.
Breaths per trial window. Teal = Evening, orange = Morning. The shaded zone left of 5 is rejected — and Morning has twice as much mass in it.
10
Fitmatches PsPM

Plant the known response shape at every accepted trial onset, add them up, and find the single scaling that best matches the measured line.

airflow_glm.py:528 fit_pooled_session_glm
one design per session, per-condition event trains basis on -10...+30 s, spm_orth, unit range convolve -> causal HP -> mean-centre -> orthogonalise centering = 1 latency = 'fixed'
The fitting machinery is faithful, including the design-matrix ordering and the −10 s pre-onset support. Its input is the problem, not its arithmetic.
One correction to what I told you earlier. I said stage 3 designed a single butter(2, band) and needed changing to match PsPM. It does not — phase1_filter_downsample already cascades two 1st-order filtfilt passes, which is exactly pspm_resp_pp.m Stage 1. There is nothing to fix there.

Scope and caveats: GLM path only; the BxB path in airflow_amp_processor.py was not examined. Counts are over the 21 subjects surviving SUBJECTS_EXCLUDE (730 Evening and 733 Morning trials). p-values are two-proportion chi-square on trial counts, with no multiplicity correction across the ten gates — the two that matter survive any reasonable correction by orders of magnitude. Nothing was changed in the pipeline to produce this page; the only edit made this session is native_envelope(), which is inert until a FORCE_RELOAD.
